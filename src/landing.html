The question this site exists to answer

Can what a system understands be made queryable, falsifiable and provenance-linked?

{{DEFINITION}} It reads the registries that already hold the stack’s truth — ComputeDriven, TRVM, the invariant factory, and Super (CD) — at pinned commits, projects them into content-addressed records, seals the result as a WallRiderLang world under a vocabulary it declares as data, and certifies the projection through TRVM. Every id is minted by the kernel that owns it. Nothing in it is a model’s opinion. Whether the graph earns its keep is the open question; what is settled is that it seals, verifies, and can be read.

What has been sealed

{{WORLDS_N}} projections, each a WallRiderLang world with a certificate.

One row per shipped projection. Bars compare nodes, relations and typed faults across the rows; the numbers beside them, and the three ids beneath, are read from each projection’s own directory. Two rows share statements: the revision id of a statement is the same in every world it appears in, and its allocation id never is — what a statement is does not depend on the world, where it is allocated does.

{{WORLDS}}

Faults are kept, not guessed away

What an adapter cannot normalize becomes a typed fault beside the records — {{FAULTS_N}} of them in the largest projection, by code. A fault is a fact about a source; a finding, {{FINDINGS}} of them on the same world, is what two normalized sources disagree about. Neither is repaired silently and neither is hidden.

{{FAULTS}}
Programmed with WallRiderLang

The program is a vocabulary, and the encoder refuses what it does not declare.

Graphonomous has no query language of its own. Its vocabulary — {{ROLES}} roles, {{KINDS}} relation kinds, {{PAIRS}} admissible (source role, target role) pairs — is a static row in WallRiderLang’s profile table, and the graph is submitted to WRL as a Semantic IR 2.0 artifact. The two-object world on the right seals. Each button applies one edit to a fresh copy and shows what WRL answered, in WRL’s own words: the code and message were produced by running the encoder, and the check runs the same six edits again.

What it reads

Seven repositories, pinned by blob, and one of them is Super.

The largest snapshot names every file it reads by commit, tree and blob, and {{ADAPTERS}} adapters read those bytes and nothing else. Super (CD) is one of the repositories, at {{SUPER_PIN}}: today the graph reads Super’s evidence at that commit and can be held to it, and that is all that is built. The intended loop is larger. Super’s blueprint gives every agent a memory that survives an engine swap, and Graphonomous is the thing meant to fill that slot: every agent under Super carrying Graphonomous as its memory, Super admitting what an agent did as receipts, and Graphonomous re-importing that admitted state deterministically. None of that runs yet, on either side — the lane is read-only by ruling until its cross-registry consistency work stops for adjudication — and this page will say so until it does.
